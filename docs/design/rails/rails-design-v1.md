# The rails, v1 — final design

Synthesised 2026-10-06 from three drafts (A "minimal mechanisms", B "scaffold first", C "loop first"), two backtest judges per draft over 40 real defects, and three adversarial passes. Spine: **Draft B** (highest backtest score, 30.3 of 40; the only draft whose kit covers layers 1, 3 and 5 as code). Grafted: Draft C's loop spine for layer 9 (the human-only channel, `hold`, `used`, the pre-grant for unattended sessions, the `gh pr merge` deny) and Draft A's structured stop reasons, gates registry, context-budget numbers and three-document template set. Every must-fix from the three adversaries is applied; where two judges disagreed, the mechanism was re-read and the verdict is stated in §12 or §14. Process language only; no household figure, account, merchant or person appears here.

---

## 1. Summary

The rails are a Claude Code plugin (`rails`, the reworked apex), a starter kit, and a one-page contract, built so that the right structure is the only one that boots, merges and ends a turn. Only four things in this toolchain can refuse: a `PreToolUse` deny, a `Stop` block, a GitHub required check, and a git hook; every rule here lives in one of those slots or is labelled judgment and kept under a tested word ceiling. The seam family (wiring, config, contract drift: 35% of 1,007 escapes) is caught by a required job that boots every real composition root, and by codegen from one declaration per setting, identifier and wire type. Domain truth is caught by one owner per concept and a real-data oracle that runs only on the operator's machine and prints rule ids, never values. Done is a signed walk receipt, not a sentence; a milestone closes only when the operator has used it for a real task. The session loop is inverted: the agent reads intent back, pushes when the local gate passes, opens one PR per line of work, arms auto-squash and watches CI itself; the operator's only words are `hold` and `used`. Every hook, job and template has a registry row with the defect it cites, its planted defect, its false-positive count and the shell and path fixtures it passed on Windows; a row that cannot be justified is deleted by a test. Household data never leaves the box: the leak check fails closed, the agent is denied any reference to the real store, and real-target evidence is written to a folder the agent cannot read. Rollout is one blocking mechanism per week, each measured before the next starts, because 19% of jarvis fixes repaired the harness and 44% of red CI jobs were the checks being wrong.

## 2. Principles

1. **Only four things can refuse.** PreToolUse deny, Stop block, required check, git hook. 22 of 32 CLAUDE.md rules were prose-only and 37% of operator corrections repeated a written rule.
2. **Boot the real root before anything else is believed.** 35.2% of escapes were seams; booting the real composition root was the cheapest catch for 130 defects, and the one job that did it gated nothing (red 37 consecutive scheduled runs while master was green).
3. **Declare once, generate the rest.** Env lists kept in four places cost 9 fixes in 7 days; the wire freeze, the one place codegen existed, was "the clearest case of contract-first working".
4. **Done is an artifact on the named target.** 208 operator catches were done-claims or screen mismatches; only 4 of 645 agent self-corrections came from looking at a screen.
5. **The harness is a product with a defect budget.** 19.3% of jarvis fixes repaired the harness; 44% of red jobs were the checks being wrong. A gate ships with its planted defect, its FP count and its Windows fixture, or not at all.
6. **Ceremony goes down, not sideways.** 33–37% of operator messages were approve/push/merge. The default is push, open, arm, watch; the operator reads two things per line of work and types `hold` only when needed.
7. **An artifact the agent writes proves nothing.** Reward-hacking literature and 17 of 20 overridden pickup denials say the shortest path to green is taken. Receipts are written by the dispatcher from what it observed and are HMAC-signed; the key and the store are deny-on-reference.
8. **Real data never enters a prompt that leaves the machine.** 21 real amounts reached git past a `$`-shaped scanner (#2527). The leak check asks the store, fails closed, and covers PR and issue bodies and subagent prompts, not only commits.

## 3. The process on one page

A **line of work** = one claim, one worktree, one branch, one PR, many commits. Gate kinds: `deny` (PreToolUse), `stop` (Stop block), `req` (GitHub required check `gate`), `git` (hook via `core.hooksPath`). Steps with no gate say `prose`.

## 4. The session contract (layer 9)

The contract is `.claude/rules/session-contract.md`, ≤600 words, always loaded, the only place the conflicting rules are resolved. Every clause names its enforcer. Hook events, exit codes and outputs are exact.

**Resolved defaults.**

| Conflict today | Resolution | Enforcer |
|---|---|---|
| "Never push without asking" vs "don't wait for my go-ahead" | Push is allowed when the `rails check` marker exists for HEAD. The operator's word is `hold`, written by the human-only channel or by `rails hold` in their own shell; `release` lifts it. Consent per push is gone; the `JARVIS_PUSH_OK` gate is deleted. | `git` pre-push (R3); `rails_prompt.py` (R24) |
| "Arm auto-merge, never poll" vs "how come you were not monitoring" | Arm at open. The monitor is `rails watch` (a background `gh pr checks`/`mergeStateStatus` poller writing `.rails/ci/<pr>.json`); the dispatcher injects a one-line state change at the next `UserPromptSubmit`/`SessionStart`. `ccd_pr set_monitor` is used when the tool exists and the operator pre-approved it; it is never a gate condition. Red wakes the session; the operator never relays. | R27 |
| "One active front" vs "do all of that work" | The front is the claimed line. `work.json` carries every item named; a turn may end with open items only with a structured `stop_reason` (`decision_needed(brief)`, `blocked(receipt)`, `hold`, `wip`). "A posture says stop" is not a reason. | R28 |
| "One issue per session" vs release scoping | The cap lives in the claim store: one defect issue per session unless the claim is a milestone being decomposed. | R23 |
| "Never full suite locally" vs "run the tests" | `rails check` runs the selector's set and the merge-tree tests for touched files; bare `pytest` with no path, `-k`, `--lf` or `-m` is denied with the alternative named; `python ci/pre_pr_check.py` is exempt by token sequence. | R2 `full_suite_gate` |

**Read intent back before the first push.** The gate is placed at push, not commit, because commits are the agent's save points and a commit deny cannot force a turn boundary. The operator sees the intent at the end of the turn that wrote it; their next message of any kind is the ack. Zero typed words are required. Unattended sessions (90 of 458) carry `rails: autonomous <issue|milestone>` in the dispatch prompt; the first `UserPromptSubmit` with that token is the ack for that scope, counted separately in the ledger so the ceremony metric is not satisfied by never asking. The residual: a sibling session's message is filtered by wrapper, but a hand-pasted relay is indistinguishable from the operator; the contract says so.

**Statements need receipts.** Done, deployed, reviewed and walked are receipts (R25). Existence, absence and root-cause claims are **prose plus a helper**: `rails whereis <term>` searches code, docs, branches, open PRs, issue titles, vault item names (via `VaultGateway` metadata; "vault unreachable" is its own outcome) and both Windows and WSL paths; `rails receipt <cmd>` records `{cmd_sha256, exit, stdout_sha256, identity}`, so the command text never reaches a PR body. The adversarial voice challenges every unscoped negative claim. A `type: prompt` Stop judge is a dated experiment in `gates.toml`, deleted at 30 days without a citation.

**Done is a walk artifact on the named target.** Two receipt kinds. `walk:planted` (CI and local) asserts values against the planted two-household corpus with `as_of` pinned in server and runner. `walk:target` runs against the local fleet root on loopback over the operator's store copy (`verify_target`, pre-merge) or the dev fleet (`used_target`, post-merge) with **structural assertions only**: no screen stuck in `loading`, no `failed`/`not_composed` on a route the intent names, every `controls.json` control responds, vocabulary ban, no 5xx. It writes step ids, pass/fail, counts, target, SHA and URL; frames and asserted text go to `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}/rails/<leaf>/evidence/`, which the dispatcher refuses to Read, Grep, Glob or reference. Fixtures, mocks, `/dev/login`, standalone and the superuser never appear in a receipt's `instrument`; `rails ship` fails a `Closes #N` whose receipt instrument is in that set, whatever the issue says.

**Gate the end of a turn.** Block up to 3 consecutive times (counter keyed on `session_id` + turn start in the claim store; then allow and write the unmet list into the SessionStart state): a `done` item whose receipt pointer does not resolve; code changed on a path this session wrote (PostToolUse log, so the operator's own edits never block) with no `check` receipt for HEAD; an armed PR whose HEAD has no matching review receipt. Block once, then pass with a loud notice: an offer-shaped ending ("want me to", "say the word") while `work.json` has open items and no `stop_reason`. A `decision_needed` reason requires a brief: problem in one line, options with costs, recommended option first and marked.

**Ceremony per PR.** Default path: zero operator messages beyond the one that follows the intent (any message). On the desktop `ccd_pr set_auto_merge` prompts unless pre-approved, so `rails ship` uses `gh pr merge --auto --squash`. Per release: one `used` record. The 878 approvals and 630 git/PR messages (dedup) are the budget this frees.

**Many sessions, one store.** Claims, open-PR index (5-minute `gh` cache), file-touch overlap, red-master owner, `hold`, `used`, receipts and the firing log live under `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}/rails/<repo-key>/`, shared by every worktree. Hooks cannot send messages; `rails_state.py` prints the overlap and the contract tells the session to `SendMessage` the sibling. Releasing claims from the main checkout is denied unless the leaf matches.

## 7. The starter kit and the retrofit path

**A new app on day one.** `rails new <app>` produces every §6.2 file, the ruleset, the plugin wiring and a walking skeleton: one root, one route with `response_model`, one screen with its state matrix, one planted household pair, one concept with an owner and an oracle, one schedule with a handler, `boot` green, the walk producing frame 1 and failing honestly on the first acceptance line. The first milestone's step 0 is "the operator does one real task on the dev target". No dossier stubs (75 of 190 were empty). The kit's own CI scaffolds an app and boots it, so the template cannot rot silently.

**Purser and jarvis, baseline-then-drain.** Every gate lands green against a frozen baseline and drains on a `drain_by` date (the `contract_gate` pattern: 93 findings → 0). One kit file is extracted per week as each mechanism lands: `roots/registry.py` (3 roots; exclusions from the #2270 class) → `settings.toml` for the fleet → `clock.py` (seven clock-seam fixes as planted cases; `PURSER_AS_OF` becomes a settings row) → `conftest.py` production-role default → `contracts/registry.py` seeded from the vocabularies that drifted → `concepts.toml` from the H1 census with the parity tests as `oracle/checker.py` → `auth_matrix.toml` for Purser and Hearth → `src/lib/load.ts` with the 42 `useLoad` sites converted screen by screen (each screen's rows are a commit) → `controls.json` and the rejecting-stub, 375 px and back/reload walk rows → `catalogue/unreached.toml` from #2348/#2400 and the 63 gaps. Drain PRs ship beside product PRs, never as a month without features; the SessionStart state prints remaining baseline rows.

## 8. Domain truth (layer 4)

**Rule tables.** Each domain concept is a row in `concepts.toml` naming its owning semantic entity and column in `apps/<app>/ontology/semantic/*.yaml` (the existing model, not a third home), its owning function, its pinning test and its oracle. The rulebook template's examples table (inputs → expected) is operator-approved before the build and uses planted values only; those examples become the pinning test. Rules the operator knows and the agent cannot infer go into `.rails/ground-truth.toml` as invariants (`non_negative`, `equals(surface)`, `sum_of(drill)`, `non_zero`), edited by the operator in their own editor.

**Golden real-data diff.** `rails oracle` runs the same checker CI runs on the planted ledger, over the operator's store, locally, and writes `rule_id, row_id (surrogate), pass`. A milestone cannot close without a zero-violation receipt at the closing SHA. The operator confirms a worked example by using the app screen (a `used` step), never by typing a value in chat.

**The privacy boundary, as denies.** The agent is denied any tool call that names the store DSN, container, `PURSER_LEAK_CHECK_STORE`, `.rails/ground-truth.toml`, `.rails/oracle/**`, the evidence folder or the receipts key (R2). A failing rule is reproduced by porting it to the planted dataset. The leak check asks the store, never a pattern, and refuses when it cannot look. Real-target walks assert structure and write values only to the denied folder. PR, issue and subagent text passes the leak check before any `gh` call or `Agent` dispatch. The milestone-close evidence is the operator's own walk, not the agent's.

## 9. Governance (layer 7)

**Earning a place.** A row in `rails/gates.toml` (app repo) or `hooks/gates.toml` (plugin); `fp_measured_on` is written by `rails gate-fp` over the last 20 merged heads and records the runner OS, so a gate calibrated on the wrong OS (Sherpa gate B) is visible in the row:

```toml
name = "boot"
kind = "ci_job"                 # ci_job | hook | lint | git_hook | arch_check | allowlist | prose
class = "friction"              # lockout | friction
prevents = ["wiring_integration", "deploy_env_config"]
cites = ["e854e76289", "c26832080a", "#2270"]
planted = "ci/planted/boot_null_seam.py"
fp_count = 1
fp_measured_on = "<sha> linux-x64"       # written by `rails gate-fp` over the last 20 merged heads
windows_fixture = "n/a"
required_by = "2026-10-27"
```

`test_gates_registry.py` fails a PR when a job or hook has no row, a field is missing, `planted` does not fail against the gate, or `required_by` has passed with the job absent from `gate.needs` — the last only on PRs touching `ci/`, `.github/`, `.claude/` or the plugin; product PRs are never blocked by a harness date. Expiries (`expires`, `drain_by`, `delete_by`) are evaluated on the schedule and printed at SessionStart; they block only a PR touching the owning path or the registry file. An extension needs `extended_reason` and an issue; `rails metrics` counts extensions per week. At most two new required gates per week; the next waits for the previous one's FP count over 20 PRs.

**Retirement.** `lockout` rows (irreversible harm: data leaving the box, shared refs, another session's tree) never expire on firing count and are deleted only by an operator-written row with a reason. `friction` rows with zero local firings in 60 days are printed at SessionStart and deleted or re-justified in a PR. A deny whose override token appears in more than half the sessions it fired in is flagged `teaches-bypass`. The yield reporter reads hook logs and CI results only; the PR Mechanisms table is narrative. Allowlists (`superuser`, `mutants`, `unreached`, `exclusions`, `leak_check`) fail `checks` when they have more rows than the merge base without an `allowlist-grow:` line in the PR body naming the row.

**Context budget: ≤6,000 tokens before the first action** (today 21–24k). CLAUDE.md ≤1,500 words (~2,000 tokens: commands and `## Judgment`); `session-contract.md` ≤600 words (~800); SessionStart state ≤600; skill listing ≤1,536 chars (~400); `MEMORY.md` index ≤100 lines (~1,500), checked by `rails check` because CI cannot see user files and the push marker binds; every other rule file is `paths:`-scoped (CI facts → `.claude/rules/ci.md` `paths: [.github/**, ci/**]`, pinned by `test_ci_lanes.py`; fleet → `rules/fleet.md`; secrets → `rules/db.md`). Subagents run `omitClaudeMd: true`.

**Require-or-delete for CI jobs.** No job runs in report-only mode without `required_by` ≤21 days. A non-required job red on 3 consecutive runs appears at SessionStart with its countdown; the question on red-at-arrival is "required or deleted", never "leave it reporting". Flake quarantine: `@quarantine(reason, until)` and `walk/quarantine.toml`, ≤8 entries, ≤7 days, tested on the bound; quarantined items run and report without reddening the job. The `--admin` bypass actor stays (operator decision, §13) and every `--admin` merge is logged as a harness defect.

**The single rulebook.** `session-contract.md` resolves conflicts (§4). `test_rule_admission.py` admits a bold rule only with its enforcer; memory notes contradicting the contract (`never push without asking`, `arm and stop`) are listed for deletion at SessionStart by a phrase grep.

## 13. Open decisions for the operator

1. **Admin bypass actor.** *(Recommended)* Keep it; deny `gh pr merge` with any flag but `--auto` in every session (R2) and log each `--admin` as a harness defect. Cost: one dispatcher rule; the browser stays your escape hatch. Alternative: remove the actor (no escape when `gate` is red for a harness reason, which is when bypass happens). Why: the habit died when the body gate did (49 → 3 → 0); the deny closes the agent's path and leaves yours.
2. **Push consent.** *(Recommended)* Delete `JARVIS_PUSH_OK`; `hold` is the only word; the leak check stays blocking and fails closed. Cost: zero per push. Alternative: consent per push (the 49% ceremony peak; the agent could set the variable itself, so it was a reminder). Why: data leaving the box is the irreversible thing, and that gate gets stronger.
3. **The intent ack.** *(Recommended)* First push waits for a turn boundary plus any message from you; `fix` lines ≤40 lines skip it; autonomous sessions carry a pre-grant token. Cost: one read per line of work, no typed word. Alternatives: a typed `go` per line (reflex within two weeks); no ack (the 170-case class stays). Why: the read-back is the message the transcripts say was missing.
4. **Review scope.** *(Recommended)* Two voices on seam paths and generated-route changes, one voice elsewhere; a must-fix needs a reproducer. Cost: 60–200k tokens and 3–10 min on roughly a third of PRs. Alternative: two voices on every PR (the token budget the adversaries priced at most PRs; 12 of 13 published review agents are below 60% signal). Why: the 17.4% catch is concentrated at seams.
5. **`boot` pricing.** *(Recommended)* Single fleet ≤10 min from the cached image, required after a 20-head FP run; two fleets on the schedule. Cost: 6–10 runner-min per armed push on 96% of PRs. Alternatives: schedule-only (the measured failure); a self-hosted runner (retired in three days last time). Why: the 35% seam family has no other instrument before merge.
6. **Merged-tree guarantee.** *(Recommended)* Local merge-tree tests + overlap-driven `update-branch` + the schedule. Cost: one `gh` call per watcher tick. Alternatives: `strict` (a rebase per merge with many open PRs); merge queue (plan-gated). Why: PR-20 is 1% of escapes.
7. **Mutation testing.** *(Recommended)* PR comment for four weeks, `not here` locally on Windows, then a per-PR survivor threshold. Cost: 1–5 min when tests/src change. Alternatives: required from day one (2 of 6 survivors were no-ops); skip (84% of test defects are mutation-catchable). Why: Google's usefulness jump came from surfacing in review, not a score.
8. **Where the plugin lives.** *(Recommended)* The apex repo renamed `rails`, local-path marketplace, user-level enablement, `rails-cli`/`rails-ci` as packages. Cost: one checkout. Alternative: inside jarvis (a second app needs the two-sources copy that drifted 88–450 lines). Why: one source, every worktree.
9. **Toolbox second-consumer rule.** *(Recommended)* Baseline the 38 with `drain_by`; enforce on new packages now. Cost: one catalogue step. Alternative: new packages only (the 1,175 zero-caller lines stay). Why: the seam share answers the brief's open question: extract on the second wired consumer.
10. **Real-data walk cadence.** *(Recommended)* Your walk per milestone close; the agent's structural `walk:target` per done claim. Cost: one real task per release. Alternative: your walk per PR (the 27%-catch instrument, but your time is the scarcest input). Why: the agent's walk removes the fixture-certifies-fixture class; yours removes the wrong-domain-fact class.

## 14. Known weaknesses and what would show they are real

1. **Semantic seam properties have no deterministic catcher.** Who may trust a stamped tenant (#362), which RLS predicate arm composes, N parallel admits at cap, one bubble per reply, a traversal path: every gate here is structural, so these fall to R26's adversarial voice. *Shown real if:* the next 40 defects shift toward negative-input and concurrency cases while the review's reproducer-backed must-fix count stays flat. *Then:* add a planted adversarial-input row to the walk and a concurrency case to the fixture rules, both as registry rows.
2. **The concept gate is list-shaped.** The one re-derivation outside the seeded census (runway, 7de1a0348f) is caught only if its figure disagrees on the planted ledger. *Shown real if:* R16 reports a violation that R15's parity test passed. *Then:* the rulebook examples table becomes mandatory for any new figure before the build.
3. **Domain truth needs the operator's time.** A concept with no approved rule table has no oracle. *Shown real if:* milestones close with `oracle` receipts covering fewer than half the user-visible figures. *Then:* `rails close` prints coverage and the operator decides per release.
4. **`boot` may repeat the fleet-smoke story.** Docker on a hosted runner, 96% of PRs, 67% historical harness-failure share. *Shown real if:* its FP count over the first 20 required runs exceeds 2. *Then:* narrow the path filter to roots and seams and keep it required; never return it to schedule-only.
5. **The Stop gate's wording tier is signage.** It blocks once; rewording passes. *Shown real if:* more than 20% of blocked turns end on the pass-through. *Then:* delete the wording tier and keep only artifact absence, as the registry rule already allows.
6. **Harness volume.** Thirty-one rows is more than apex shipped working; the PowerShell tokeniser, the static reachability walker and the Testcontainers default are new code on a box with recurring Docker deaths and disk fills. *Shown real if:* the harness share of red jobs rises above 44% in weeks 1–6 or `fix(ci)` commits exceed 20% of the month. *Then:* the next mechanism waits; the registry's cap is already two per week.
7. **Receipts are signed, not true.** A `whereis` against the wrong tree still yields a receipt; the identity line is the only defence. *Shown real if:* a retraction cites a receipted claim. *Then:* the identity line gains the host, cwd and tree hash the retraction lacked.
8. **The ack channel has a sibling-session residual.** A relayed message pasted by hand is indistinguishable from the operator. *Shown real if:* a misread-intent correction follows an ack that came from a relay. *Then:* the SessionStart state prints which message acked which intent.
9. **Governance has never run.** No mechanism in either repo was ever deleted because a ledger said so. *Shown real if:* at day 60 `gate_yield.json` lists zero-yield rows and none has been deleted. *Then:* the design has failed on its own terms, and the proof it asks for is one deletion.
10. **Cost is estimated, not measured.** Every per-PR figure here is priced from workflow timeouts and transcript counts. *Shown real if:* `rails metrics` reports monthly CI minutes above the plan's limit or agent wall per PR above 30 minutes on one-line fixes. *Then:* `boot` and `walk` move to armed-only (already the design) and the review filter narrows further.
