# The rails, v1.1 — final design, revised once

v1 was synthesised 2026-10-06 from three drafts (A "minimal mechanisms", B "scaffold first", C "loop first"), two backtest judges per draft over 40 real defects and three adversarial passes; spine Draft B, grafts from C and A. v1.1 is the one revision: it answers the final backtest (21 yes / 18 partial / 1 no; 14 of the 21 are cases a row already cites) and the final adversary's `adopt_with_fixes`. Every must-fix is applied, every should-fix is applied or scoped in §14, and §15 lists each change against its finding. Process language only; no household figure, account, merchant or person appears here.

---

## 1. Summary

The rails are a Claude Code plugin (`rails`, the reworked apex), a starter kit, and a one-page contract, built so that the right structure is the only one that boots, merges and ends a turn. Only four things can refuse: a `PreToolUse` deny, a `Stop` block, a GitHub required check, and a git hook; every rule lives in one of those slots or is labelled judgment under a tested word ceiling. The seam family (35% of 1,007 escapes) is caught by a required job that boots every real composition root on every PR whose paths touch it, and by codegen from one declaration per setting, identifier and wire type. Where v1 depended on the agent remembering to register a thing, v1.1 derives the check from an artifact that already exists: the router, the database catalog, the semantic YAML, the JSX, the coverage trace. Domain truth is caught by one owner per concept, a parity checker independent of every surface, and a real-data oracle that runs at ship time and prints rule ids, never values. Done is a walk receipt whose step ids match the acceptance lines; a milestone closes only when the operator has used it for a real task. The session loop is inverted: the agent shows intent and reads the reply, pushes when the local gate passes, opens one PR per line of work, arms auto-squash and binds a monitor; the operator's words are `hold`, `used` and `approve`. Receipts are tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. Every hook, job and template has a registry row with the defect it cites, its planted defect, its FP count and its Windows fixtures. Household data never leaves the box: the leak check compares against an operator-refreshed snapshot of store values and fails closed, the agent is denied any reference to the real store by name, and real-target evidence goes to a folder the agent cannot read. Rollout is one blocking mechanism per week, each in shadow mode before it denies.

## 2. Principles

1. **Only four things can refuse.** PreToolUse deny, Stop block, required check, git hook. 22 of 32 CLAUDE.md rules were prose-only; 37% of corrections repeated a written rule.
2. **Boot the real root before anything else is believed.** 35.2% of escapes were seams; the one job that booted the real root gated nothing.
3. **Declare once, generate the rest.** Env lists kept in four places cost 9 fixes in 7 days; the wire freeze was the clearest case of contract-first working.
4. **Done is an artifact on the named target.** 208 operator catches were done-claims or screen mismatches; 4 of 645 agent self-corrections came from looking at a screen.
5. **The harness is a product with a defect budget.** 19.3% of fixes repaired the harness; 44% of red jobs were the checks being wrong. A gate ships with its planted defect, FP count and Windows fixture, or not at all; a harness row red on arrival is reverted.
6. **Ceremony goes down, not sideways.** 33–37% of operator messages were approve/push/merge. A gate whose discharge is slower than its bypass teaches the bypass, so nothing here blocks per commit.
7. **An artifact the agent writes proves nothing by itself.** Receipts are written by the dispatcher from what it observed and carry an integrity HMAC; on a single-user box the key is one indirection away, so forging is made **detectable** (`forged` = a receipt with no matching hook firing), not impossible.
8. **Real data never enters a prompt that leaves the machine.** 21 real amounts reached git past a `$`-shaped scanner (#2527). The leak check compares against store values, fails closed, and is keyed on destination, not verb.
9. **Derive the check from an artifact that must already exist.** The backtest's partials were catches contingent on a row the agent had to add. A lint forces the registration (a literal bound, an unregistered figure, an unlabelled control) or a generator skips it (routes from the router, RLS tables from the catalog, reach from coverage).

## 4. The session contract (layer 9)

`.claude/rules/session-contract.md`, ≤600 words, always loaded, the only place the conflicting rules are resolved. Every clause names its enforcer.

**Resolved defaults.**

| Conflict today | Resolution | Enforcer |
|---|---|---|
| "Never push without asking" vs "don't wait for my go-ahead" | Push is allowed when the marker exists for HEAD. The operator's word is `hold` (human-only channel or `rails hold` in their shell); `release` lifts it; `RAILS_OPERATOR=1` in their shell skips marker and hold. `JARVIS_PUSH_OK` is deleted. | `git` pre-push (R3); R24 |
| "Arm auto-merge, never poll" vs "how come you were not monitoring" | Arm at open and bind a monitor in the same command: `ccd_pr bind_pr` + `set_monitor` when the tool exists, else a poller launched with `Win32_Process Create` (a `&`/`Start-Process` child is reaped on this box). Red or green is injected at the next prompt, or earlier where the monitor can wake the session. An armed PR with no monitor receipt is a Stop artifact absence. | R27, R28 |
| "One active front" vs "do all of that work" | The front is the claimed line. `work.json` carries every item named; a turn may end with open items only with a structured `stop_reason` (`decision_needed(brief)`, `blocked(receipt)`, `hold`, `wip`). | R28 |
| "One issue per session" vs release scoping | One defect issue per session unless the claim is a milestone being decomposed; the cap lives in the claim store. | R23 |

**Read intent back before the first push.** The gate is at push, not commit: commits are the agent's save points and a commit deny cannot force a turn boundary. The operator sees the intent in the final message of the turn that wrote it (the Stop hook refuses to stamp one it cannot find there); their next message acks it unless it changes what is built, in which case the intent is rewritten and shown again. Unattended sessions (90 of 458) carry `rails: autonomous <scope>`, counted separately so the ceremony metric is not satisfied by never asking. Residual: a hand-pasted relay is indistinguishable from the operator; the contract says so.

**Statements need receipts.** Done, deployed, reviewed and walked are receipts (R25). Existence, absence and root-cause claims are prose plus a helper: `rails whereis <term>` searches code, docs, branches, open PRs, issue titles, vault item names (metadata only; "vault unreachable" is its own outcome) and Windows and WSL paths; `rails receipt <cmd>` records `{cmd_sha256, exit, stdout_sha256, identity}`. One mechanical check: `rails ship` fails a body or intent line containing an absence word (`no`, `never`, `missing`, `does not exist`, `only`) with no `receipt:` id on the same line.

**Done is a walk artifact on the named target.** Acceptance lines carry a `step:` id. `walk:planted` (CI and local) asserts values against the planted two-household corpus with `as_of` pinned in server and runner. `walk:target` runs against the local fleet root over the operator's store copy (`verify_target`, pre-merge) or the dev fleet (`used_target`, post-merge) with structural assertions only: no screen stuck in `loading`, no `failed`/`not_composed` on a route the intent names, every `controls.json` control responds, `controls.json ⊇ elements.json`, vocabulary ban, no 5xx. Both kinds list step ids with pass/fail; an item closes only when its step id is `pass` in a receipt whose instrument is allowed. All child stdout/stderr goes to `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}/rails/<leaf>/evidence/`, which the dispatcher refuses to Read, Grep, Glob or reference; the tool output is the receipt schema only, and a fixture plants a failing step and asserts no asserted text reaches stdout. Fixtures, mocks, `/dev/login`, standalone and the superuser never appear in an `instrument`; `rails ship` fails a `Closes #N` whose receipt instrument is in that set.

**Gate the end of a turn.** Two artifact conditions, each blocking up to 3 consecutive times: a `done` item whose step id has no passing receipt; an armed PR whose HEAD has no matching review receipt or no monitor receipt. On the third block the turn ends and `unverified: [items]` is written into `work.json`; `rails ship` refuses a `Closes #N` while any item is `unverified`, and `rails_state.py` prints them — the pass-through is a recorded debt, not a free exit. There is no per-turn `check` condition: the push marker binds the check to the only moment it matters. An offer-shaped ending ("want me to", "say the word") with open items and no `stop_reason` blocks once. A `decision_needed` reason requires a brief: problem in one line, options with costs, recommended first and marked.

**Ceremony per PR.** Zero operator messages beyond the one that follows the intent; `rails ship` arms with `gh pr merge --auto --squash` (the desktop's `ccd_pr set_auto_merge` prompts unless pre-approved). Per release: `used` and `approve`. The 878 approvals and 630 git/PR messages (dedup) are the budget this frees.

**Many sessions, one store.** Claims, open-PR index (5-minute `gh` cache), file-touch overlap, red-master owner, `hold`, `used`, `approve`, receipts and the firing log live under `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}/rails/<repo-key>/`, shared by every worktree under `_claimstore.py`'s lock. `rails_state.py` prints the overlap and the contract tells the session to `SendMessage` the sibling. Releasing claims from the main checkout is denied unless the leaf matches.

## 7. The starter kit and the retrofit path

**A new app on day one.** `rails new <app>` produces every §6.2 file, the ruleset, the plugin wiring and a walking skeleton: one root, one route with `response_model`, one screen with its state matrix and `data-control` ids, one planted household pair, one concept with an owner, an examples table and an oracle, one `limits.py` cap with its generated parallel test, one schedule with a handler, `boot` green, the walk failing honestly on the first acceptance line. **The skeleton is scaffolded in week 1** so the kit's own CI boots from day one; Purser-specific files are extracted into it as each wall lands. The first milestone's step 0 is "the operator does one real task on the dev target". No dossier stubs (75 of 190 were empty).

**Purser and jarvis, baseline-then-drain.** Every gate lands green against a frozen baseline and drains on a `drain_by` date (the `contract_gate` pattern: 93 findings → 0). One kit file per week as each mechanism lands: `roots/registry.py` → `settings.toml` → `clock.py` (`PURSER_AS_OF` becomes a settings row) → `conftest.py` with the generated RLS and cap tests → `contracts/registry.py` from the vocabularies that drifted → `concepts.toml` from the H1 census and every numeric YAML column → `limits.py` from the `limits_gate` baseline → `auth_matrix.toml` → `src/lib/load.ts` and `Figure`, screen by screen → `data-control` ids and `elements.json` → `catalogue/unreached.toml` from #2348/#2400 and the 63 gaps. Drain PRs ship beside product PRs, never as a month without features. The catch rate on the existing tree starts below the backtest's 21/40 because the Purser instances of #2240, #2348 and #2531 would be baselined rows; the drain order puts the three largest classes first for that reason.

## 8. Domain truth (layer 4)

**Rule tables.** Each domain concept is a row in `concepts.toml` naming its owning semantic entity and column in `apps/<app>/ontology/semantic/*.yaml` (the existing model, not a third home), its owning function, its pinning test, its oracle and its examples table (planted values only). Every numeric YAML column needs a row, so a new figure cannot ship unregistered; the examples table is agent-written and becomes the pinning test at once, and the operator pins it with `rails approve <rulebook> <examples_hash>` before the milestone closes — the only channel through which "operator-reviewed data" means anything. Rules the operator knows and the agent cannot infer go into `.rails/ground-truth.toml` as invariants (`non_negative`, `equals(surface)`, `sum_of(drill)`, `non_zero`), edited in their own editor.

**Golden real-data diff.** `rails oracle` runs the checker CI runs on the planted ledger over the operator's store, locally, and writes `rule_id, row_id (surrogate), pass`; its child output goes to the denied folder, so the agent runs it at `rails ship` when an owner changed, and a milestone cannot close without a zero-violation receipt at the closing SHA. The operator confirms a worked example by using the app screen (a `used` step), never by typing a value in chat. Golden sets for model features are planted only in CI; a real-input set lives under the denied folder and is replayed only by `rails oracle --live` from the operator's shell.

**The privacy boundary, as denies.** The agent is denied any tool call that names the store DSN, container or database names, `PURSER_LEAK_CHECK_STORE`, `.rails/{ground-truth.toml,oracle,snapshot}`, the evidence folder or the receipts key (R2); planted and dev databases carry the `rails_planted_` prefix and a leased port, so a `psql` against the shared instance has a deny token to hit. A failing rule is reproduced by porting it to the planted dataset. The leak check compares against the value snapshot, never a pattern, and refuses when the snapshot is missing or stale. Real-target walks and the oracle write values only to the denied folder and print the receipt schema. PR, issue and subagent text passes the leak check before any body-carrying `gh` call or `Agent` dispatch.

## 9. Governance (layer 7)

**Earning a place.** A row in `rails/gates.toml` (app repo) or `hooks/gates.toml` (plugin); `fp_measured_on` is written by `rails gate-fp` over the last 20 merged heads and records the runner OS, so a gate calibrated on the wrong OS (Sherpa gate B) is visible:

```toml
[[gate]]
name = "boot"
kind = "ci_job"                 # ci_job | hook | lint | git_hook | arch_check | allowlist | prose
class = "friction"              # lockout | friction
prevents = ["wiring_integration", "deploy_env_config"]
cites = ["e854e76289", "c26832080a", "#2270"]
planted = "ci/planted/boot_null_seam.py"
fp_count = 1
fp_measured_on = "<sha> linux-x64"
duration_p50_p95 = "7m10s 9m40s"  # week 1, three workflow_dispatch runs
shadow_until = ""                 # denies only: 7 days log-only before promotion
required_by = "2026-10-27"
```

`test_gates_registry.py` fails a PR when a job or hook has no row, a field is missing, `planted` does not fail against the gate, or `required_by` has passed with the job absent from `gate.needs` — the last only on PRs touching `ci/`, `.github/`, `.claude/` or the plugin; the planted run is scoped to rows whose paths are in the diff, the full matrix runs on the schedule. Expiries are evaluated on the schedule and printed at SessionStart; they block only a PR touching the owning path or the registry. An extension needs `extended_reason` and an issue. At most two new required gates per week; a new deny runs 7 days in shadow first; a harness row red on arrival is reverted, not fixed forward.

**Retirement.** `lockout` rows (data leaving the box, shared refs, another session's tree) never expire on firing count and are deleted only by an operator-written row with a reason. `friction` rows with zero local firings in 60 days are printed at SessionStart and deleted or re-justified. A deny whose override token appears in more than half the sessions it fired in (a quarter, for the one-PR row) is flagged `teaches-bypass`. **Allowlists** (`superuser`, `mutants`, `unreached`, `exclusions`, `leak_check`, `skip_ok`) carry their justification in the row — `why=`, `added_in=<issue>`, `expires=` — and `checks` fails only a new row missing those fields; nothing is keyed on PR-body prose, the pattern that produced 49 `--admin` merges.

**Context budget: ≤6,000 tokens before the first action** (today 21–24k). CLAUDE.md ≤1,500 words; `session-contract.md` ≤600 words; SessionStart state ≤600 tokens; skill listing ≤1,536 chars; `MEMORY.md` index ≤100 lines as a notice; every other rule file is `paths:`-scoped (`rules/ci.md`, `rules/fleet.md`, `rules/db.md`). Subagents run `omitClaudeMd: true`.

**Require-or-delete for CI jobs.** No job runs report-only without `required_by` ≤21 days; a non-required job red on 3 consecutive runs appears at SessionStart with its countdown, and the question is "required or deleted". Flake quarantine: `@quarantine(reason, until)` and `walk/quarantine.toml`, ≤8 entries, ≤7 days, tested on the bound. The `--admin` bypass actor stays (§13); every `--admin` merge is logged as a harness defect. **Spend**: `[budget]` names `included_minutes` and `spend_limit`; `rails metrics` prints month-to-date at SessionStart above 70%, `mutate-changed` moves to the schedule past that line, and spend-limit exhaustion is its own `run_triage` exit code.

**The single rulebook.** `session-contract.md` resolves conflicts (§4). `test_rule_admission.py` lints modal lines outside `## Judgment` for their enforcer, under the word ceiling that does the real work; memory notes contradicting the contract are listed for deletion at SessionStart by a phrase grep.

## 13. Open decisions for the operator

1. **Admin bypass actor.** *(Recommended)* Keep it; deny every agent merge path by effect (R2), log each `--admin` as a harness defect, verify in week 1 with a ruleset read and a dry-run push. Alternative: remove the actor (no escape when `gate` is red for a harness reason, which is when bypass happens). Why: the habit died when the body gate did (49 → 3 → 0).
2. **Push consent.** *(Recommended)* Delete `JARVIS_PUSH_OK`; `hold` is the only word; `RAILS_OPERATOR=1` in your own shell skips the marker, never the leak check. Alternative: consent per push (the 49% ceremony peak). Why: data leaving the box is the irreversible thing, and that gate gets stronger.
3. **The intent ack.** *(Recommended)* First push waits for a shown intent plus your next message, with a one-call classifier that treats a correction as a non-ack; `fix` lines ≤40 lines skip it; autonomous sessions carry a pre-grant token. Alternatives: a typed `go` per line (reflex within two weeks); any-message ack (a "no, do X" would ack the wrong intent). Why: the read-back was the missing message; the correction must not count.
4. **Review scope.** *(Recommended)* Two voices on seam paths, generated-route changes and concurrency/transaction diffs; one voice elsewhere; a must-fix needs a reproducer. Cost: 60–200k tokens and 3–10 min on roughly a third of PRs. Alternative: two voices everywhere. Why: the 17.4% catch concentrates at seams; the backtest's one `no` was a concurrency case.
5. **`boot` and `walk` on path filters, every run after PR open.** *(Recommended)* Per-PR cancellation, `needs: checks`, `gate` fails rather than skips when the filter matched. Cost: +10–21 runner-min per run. Alternatives: armed-only (the v1 hole: a required check the first arm on a green SHA never runs); schedule-only (the measured failure). Why: pre-PR pushes are free, so the cost lands only where a merge is in prospect.
6. **CI minutes.** *(Recommended)* Set `spend_limit` to cover the projected 4,000–10,000 min/month and pay the overage (≈$0.008 per Linux minute at list, verified in week 1); `mutate-changed` to the schedule past 70%. Alternatives: cut `suite-db` to the schedule (the 43% of the suite once unrun before merge); a self-hosted runner (retired in three days last time). Why: a stopped run read as a lint failure cost more than the minutes.
7. **Merged-tree guarantee and mutation.** *(Recommended)* Lint on the merge tree, tests on the working tree, overlap-driven `update-branch` + disarm, the schedule as backstop; mutation as a PR comment for four weeks then a threshold. Alternatives: `strict` (a rebase per merge); merge queue; mutation required from day one (2 of 6 survivors were no-ops). Why: PR-20 is 1% of escapes; mutation's value is in review, not a score.
8. **Where the plugin lives.** *(Recommended)* The apex repo renamed `rails`, local-path marketplace, user-level enablement, `rails-cli`/`rails-ci` as path dependencies on that checkout. Alternative: inside jarvis (the two-sources copy drifted 88–450 lines). Why: one source, every worktree, hooks and CI from one tree.
9. **Toolbox second-consumer rule.** *(Recommended)* Baseline the 38 with `drain_by`; enforce on new packages now. Alternative: new packages only (the 1,175 zero-caller lines stay). Why: extract on the second executed consumer.
10. **Real-data cadence.** *(Recommended)* The agent runs `rails oracle` at ship when an owner changed and `walk --target` per done claim; your walk and `approve` per milestone close. Alternative: your walk per PR (the 27%-catch instrument, but your time is the scarcest input). Why: the agent's instruments remove fixture-certifies-fixture and surface disagreement; yours removes wrong domain facts.

## 14. Known weaknesses and what would show they are real

1. **Semantic seam properties still have no complete deterministic catcher.** v1.1 generates what an existing artifact allows (RLS under unbound and empty-string sessions, `cap+3` admits, traversal inputs, no-op handlers, one bubble per `chat_send`); the rest (who may trust a stamped tenant, rollback semantics, proxy-peer keying) goes to the Seams table and R26's second voice. *Real if:* the next 40 defects shift behavioural while reproducer-backed must-fixes stay flat. *Then:* a planted adversarial-input walk row per screen.
2. **The checker is agent-written.** An unregistered figure cannot ship; a formula missing a term is caught only if `oracle/checker.py` disagrees, and the agent wrote both; `approve` binds at close. *Real if:* R16 fails a concept whose planted parity passed. *Then:* `approve` moves to the first PR touching the concept.
3. **Domain truth needs the operator's time.** *Real if:* milestones close with `approve` covering under half the visible figures. *Then:* `rails close` prints coverage; the operator decides.
4. **`boot` may repeat the fleet-smoke story.** *Real if:* FP over the first 20 required runs exceeds 2, or week-1 p95 exceeds 10 min. *Then:* narrow the filter to roots and seams; never schedule-only.
5. **The Stop gate's wording tier is signage; the artifact tier has a recorded exit.** *Real if:* more than 20% of blocked turns pass through, or `unverified` is cleared by editing `work.json`. *Then:* delete the wording tier; `work.json` becomes dispatcher-written only.
6. **Harness volume.** The tokeniser, the coverage census and the Testcontainers default are new code on a box with recurring Docker deaths; mitigations: shadow mode, scoped planted runs, measured durations, the snapshot leak check, revert-on-arrival. *Real if:* harness share of red jobs rises above 44% in weeks 1–6 or `fix(ci)` exceeds 20% of commits. *Then:* the next mechanism waits.
7. **Receipts are tamper-evident, not true.** *Real if:* a retraction cites a receipted claim, or `forged` is ever non-zero. *Then:* the identity line gains host, cwd and tree hash; a `forged` session loses `--adhoc` for a week.
8. **The ack channel has residuals.** A pasted relay is indistinguishable from the operator; the classifier will misread some corrections. *Real if:* a misread-intent correction follows an ack attributed to a relay or a correction-shaped message. *Then:* `release`-tier intents require the operator's own `rails ack`.
9. **Governance has never run.** *Real if:* at day 60 `gate_yield.json` lists zero-yield rows and none has been deleted. *Then:* the design has failed on its own terms.
10. **Cost is estimated and already over the quota.** The §5 totals assume today's job durations and 1–3 runs per PR. *Real if:* monthly minutes exceed `spend_limit` or agent wall per PR exceeds 30 min on one-line fixes. *Then:* `walk` narrows to touched screens; the review filter narrows.
11. **The backtest is partly a self-test.** 14 of the 21 `yes` verdicts are cases a row cites; R8's removal list, R12's vocabulary ban and R10's `when_for(None)` are defects written as rules. v1.1 answers with generators over existing artifacts; the only honest test is the next one. *Real if:* 40 defects not cited by any row score below 15 `yes`. *Then:* list-shaped rows are re-cut as generators or deleted.
12. **Not worth catching deterministically, by decision.** A golden input that happens to be a join (9f5fa69c35) is authoring, not a gate; a stale model recording passing in CI is the price of not paying and not leaving the box on every PR; a `used` that stalls every session is answered by the `prep` claim.
13. **Should-fixes refused: none; two scoped down.** The Stop hook's monitor condition requires a monitor *bound*, not CI green (waiting on CI at turn end would reintroduce polling); the adhoc-by-app refusal exempts operator-created lines, the case `--adhoc` exists for.
