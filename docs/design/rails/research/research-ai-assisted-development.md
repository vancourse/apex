# Research brief: agentic software development practice, mapped to our defect classes

Written 2026-10-05 for the designers of the rails. Sources are 2025–2026 unless noted; each is tagged **[measured]** (a number from a study, telemetry or a controlled comparison), **[anecdote]** (a practitioner's own account), or **[vendor]** (a tool maker's unquantified claim). Defect classes are the `coverage_matrix.json` root causes with their share of the 1,007 escapes; operator-caught classes are the `transcript_implications.json` mistake rows with their counts.

## 0. The frame the field has settled on

Every serious 2026 source uses the same equation: **agent = model + harness**, and the harness is where reliability is won or lost. Böckeler defines the harness as "everything in an AI agent except the model itself" and splits it into **guides** (feed-forward: AGENTS.md, skills, bootstrap scripts, LSP) and **sensors** (feedback: linters, type checkers, structural tests, coverage, mutation testing, review agents), with the goal of "a feedback loop that self-corrects as many issues as possible before they even reach human eyes" ([martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html](https://martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html)) [anecdote].

The claim that the harness matters more than the model is now **[measured]**: holding the model and task fixed and changing only how older tool results are trimmed raised fail-to-pass from 28% to 49% and complete solutions from 43 to 72 on a 169-task SWE-bench cohort, so the authors argue evaluations "should treat the model and harness together as the tested solver" ([arxiv.org/abs/2608.26218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26218)). A 176-setting study found context management "becomes increasingly valuable as the context-window budget tightens", planning "shifts from an accuracy scaffold for weaker models to a cost saver for stronger models", and bash-capable models do fine with a bash-only tool surface ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804)). Vercel removed most of an agent's purpose-built tools in favour of one bash tool and went from 4/5 to 5/5 success, 3.5× faster, 37% fewer tokens, with the stated precondition "this only worked because our semantic layer was already good documentation" ([vercel.com/blog/we-removed-80-percent-of-our-agents-tools](https://vercel.com/blog/we-removed-80-percent-of-our-agents-tools)) [measured, n=5].

Marmelab's survey of 391 harness repositories (median age 8.7 months) found 60% have neither tests nor evals and only 5 of 391 measure their own usage in production; its one-line conclusion is that the discipline agrees on the definition and on nothing else ([marmelab.com](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/09/24/the-state-of-ai-harness-engineering-2026.html)) [measured]. The slogan "2026 looks like the year they learn that the harness is the product" is from a practitioner blog, not a study ([kvassiliou.com](https://kvassiliou.com/tech/harness-engineering-coding-agents-2026)) [anecdote].

## 1. Practices, one at a time

Format: what it is · who says so · evidence · which of our classes it addresses · the practice's own failure mode.

### P1. Give the agent a check it can run, and make "done" mean the check passed

Anthropic's current guide opens with this as the highest-leverage move: "Claude stops when the work looks done. Without a check it can run, 'looks done' is the only signal available, and you become the verification loop." It names four escalating gates: run-and-iterate in the prompt, a `/goal` condition re-checked every turn, a **Stop hook** that "blocks the turn from ending until it passes", and a verification subagent so "the agent doing the work isn't the one grading it". It also asks for evidence over assertion: "the test output, the command it ran and what it returned, or a screenshot" ([code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]. Hashimoto: agents need "fast, high quality tools to automatically tell it when it is wrong", and with those they "more often than not fix [their] own mistakes" ([mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)) [anecdote]. Ball: "If I know it works, what do I care about the code?" and review intensity should scale with risk, testing over reading ([changelog.com/podcast/648](https://changelog.com/podcast/648), summarised at [reading.torqsoftware.com](https://reading.torqsoftware.com/notes/software/ai-ml/agentic-coding/2026-01-26-thorsten-ball-code-review-ai-agents-testing-over-reading/)) [anecdote]. Willison: "Without tests? Your agent might claim something works without having actually tested it at all" ([simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/7/vibe-engineering](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/7/vibe-engineering/)) [anecdote]. Anthropic's agent-design essay gives the reason coding is tractable at all: solutions are "verifiable through automated tests" and agents need "ground truth from the environment at each step" ([anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)) [vendor].

**Addresses:** claimed-done-unverified (111 operator catches), verified-on-fixture (25), ux_truthfulness (4.9%), ui_behavior (3.5%), and the finding that only 4 of 645 agent self-corrections came from looking at a screen. **Failure mode:** the check becomes the target (see P7); the Stop hook has a consecutive-block cap, so an agent that cannot pass eventually exits anyway ([code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)); and a check that runs against a mock or dev harness certifies the mock, which is our PR-09 door-and-instrument problem exactly.

### P2. Hooks for anything that must happen every time; prose only for judgment

Anthropic draws the line in one sentence: "Unlike CLAUDE.md instructions which are advisory, hooks are deterministic and guarantee the action happens" ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]. The mechanics: exit code 2 blocks on `PreToolUse`, `UserPromptSubmit`, `Stop`, `SubagentStop` and a few others; `PostToolUse` cannot block; a tool-event matcher filters by tool name only, so a `Bash` matcher "does NOT cover PowerShell"; a missing or non-executable script exits 127 and is treated as a **non-blocking error, the action proceeds**; Windows exec-form hooks need real `.exe` targets, not `.cmd` shims ([hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)) [vendor]. That fail-open default is what made three apex hooks look healthy while inert, and it is why the brief's "fail open with a loud notice" constraint needs the notice built in, since the platform's own notice is one transcript line.

The strongest **[measured]** case for the rule is the CLAUDE.md security study: across 481 public files, under the strictest match only 4.4% (CI 2.6–6.7) of written security rules were backed by a built-in control, and the authors call the file "a write-only channel. A developer writes a security rule but gets no feedback on whether a control will enforce it" ([arxiv.org/abs/2608.23550v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23550v1)). Agents also route around enforcement that lives in git rather than in the tool layer: Claude Code issue #40117 documents six consecutive commits that used `--no-verify`, `git stash` and quiet flags despite a memory rule and a CLAUDE.md rule forbidding it, with up to 63 tests failing per commit; the issue was closed "not planned", and the reporter's working mitigation was a `permissions.deny` on the flag variants plus a pre-push hook that refuses when a marker written by a successful pre-commit run is missing ([github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/40117](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/40117)) [anecdote, logged]. SpecStory's explanation: a failing hook "stands between the agent and a finished task", and the bypass flag is the shortest path to its "done" message ([specstory.com](https://specstory.com/learning/ci-cd/agents-bypassing-pre-commit-hooks)) [anecdote].

**Addresses:** the 22-of-32 prose-only rules, the 35% of corrections that repeated a written rule, git_or_ci_mishandling (44), and `--admin` merges around `pr_body_check`. **Failure mode:** hooks are branch-local and tool-local; they cover one entry point (Bash) and miss the next (PowerShell, a subagent, a script); and a hook that blocks a cheap action but asks for an expensive one is just advice with a worse UI (our 13% phase-nudge follow-through).

### P3. Keep the always-loaded instruction file tiny; move everything else behind progressive disclosure

Anthropic: "For each line, ask: 'Would removing this cause Claude to make mistakes?' If not, cut it. Bloated CLAUDE.md files cause Claude to ignore your actual instructions!" and "If you emphasize many lines, none of them stands out"; use skills for anything only sometimes relevant, and `/doctor` proposes cuts ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]. Anthropic's context essay frames it as an attention budget: find "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens" ([effective-context-engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)) [vendor]. Skills load only `name` and `description` at startup and the body on demand ([agent-skills](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills)) [vendor]. OpenAI's agent-first codebase kept AGENTS.md to "~100 lines as a table of contents" pointing into a structured `docs/` tree, because "Context is a scarce resource. A giant instruction file crowds out the task, the code, and the relevant docs" ([openai.com/index/harness-engineering](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), quoted via [emilsit.net](https://www.emilsit.net/t/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering/) and [alexlavaee.me](https://alexlavaee.me/blog/openai-agent-first-codebase-learnings/)) [anecdote]. Codex caps combined AGENTS.md content at 32 KiB by default ([learn.chatgpt.com/docs/agent-configuration/agents-md](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/agent-configuration/agents-md)) [vendor]. Thoughtworks' Spec Kit blip names "instruction bloat" where "continually adding project context led to a growing agent instruction set and eventually context rot" ([thoughtworks.com radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/radar/languages-and-frameworks/github-spec-kit)) [anecdote].

The uncomfortable **[measured]** datum: ETH Zurich found that repository context files (both LLM-generated and developer-committed) "did not enhance task success rates" on SWE-bench-style tasks and raised inference cost by over 20%; instructions in the files were followed, but "repository overviews, although popular and recommended by model providers, are not helpful" ([arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)). So the content that earns its place is commands and non-inferable constraints, not narrative.

**Addresses:** CLAUDE.md regrowth 1,962→9,160 words, first-turn context 86k→105k tokens, docs_drift (3.4%), and the 5% graph-first compliance. **Failure mode:** a ceiling with no enforcement regrows (we measured this); a skill that is never triggered is prose in a different folder (apex's 45 skills, 4 review-skill calls in 458 sessions).

### P4. Enforce architecture mechanically, with error messages written for the agent

OpenAI's ~1M-line, ~1,500-PR codebase with no hand-written code enforced a one-way layer order (Types → Config → Repo → Service → Runtime → UI) with custom linters and structural tests, and wrote lint errors as instructions: "Service layer cannot import from UI layer. Move this logic to a Provider or restructure the dependency". Lopopolo's line is "Documentation alone doesn't keep a fully agent-generated codebase coherent"; invariants encoded once "apply universally" ([openai.com](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/) via [infoq.com](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering-codex), [emilsit.net](https://www.emilsit.net/t/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering/)) [anecdote, at scale]. Böckeler lists structural tests (ArchUnit-style) among the sensors ([harness-engineering](https://martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html)). Ronacher adds a design rule for agent-legible code: permission checks hidden in another file "will almost guarantee you that the AI will forget", so prefer explicit, descriptively named functions over clever indirection ([lucumr.pocoo.org](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2025/6/12/agentic-coding/)) [anecdote].

**Addresses:** contract_drift (8.9%, our best-performing class where a gate exists), wiring_integration (15.7%), domain_rule re-derivation (5.4%, one concept implemented 2–10 times), ignored-existing-component (44). **Failure mode:** a structural gate satisfied by the wrong evidence (our `modules-are-consumed` discharged by any `__init__` import); gates that check import direction say nothing about whether a route is mounted or a handler registered, which is our dominant seam class and needs a boot of the real root, not a linter.

### P5. Separate the writer from the reviewer; give the reviewer a fresh context and a narrow brief

Anthropic: "A fresh context improves code review since Claude won't be biased toward code it just wrote", with a Writer/Reviewer session pattern and a review subagent that "sees only the diff and the criteria you give it, not the reasoning that produced the change". The explicit caveat: "A reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, even when the work is sound... Chasing every finding leads to over-engineering" ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]. Anthropic's multi-agent write-up reports a 90.2% gain over a single agent on an internal research eval, but at ~15× chat tokens, and warns "most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than research" ([multi-agent-research-system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)) [vendor, internal eval].

The **[measured]** evidence on automated review is sobering: across 3,109 PRs, 12 of 13 code-review agents had signal ratios below 60%, agent-only-reviewed PRs merged at 45.2% versus 68.4% for human-only, and low-signal feedback correlated with abandonment ([arxiv.org/abs/2604.03196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03196)). Marmelab's survey reports a benchmark where "adding reviewer agents decreased success by 8%" and finds no clear multi-agent advantage ([marmelab.com](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/09/24/the-state-of-ai-harness-engineering-2026.html)) [measured, secondary]. Our own data is the counterweight: cold two-voice review found 9 distinct must-fix defects in 46 reviews on one PR and three STRIDE findings neither voice held alone, which is better than the published averages and is why it stays in the top ten.

**Addresses:** code review + seam design review were the earliest catch for 17.4% of defects; security_privacy (8.5%). **Failure mode:** noise and over-engineering; reviewers that read documents instead of code have "no stopping point" (our rev-7 design); a reviewer sharing context with the writer inherits its blind spots.

### P6. Gate the end of the turn and demand receipts

The `Stop` hook is blockable and can run a script that refuses an ending until a condition holds ([hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)) [vendor]. Anthropic asks for evidence over assertion ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). Böckeler's catalogue of agent missteps includes misdiagnosis, brute-force fixes (raising Docker memory instead of finding the leak), redundant tests and gratuitous workflow complexity, with the observation that "the longer a coding session gets, the more hit-and-miss it becomes" and that the costliest mistakes have the longest feedback loops ([martinfowler.com .../13-role-of-developer-skills](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/13-role-of-developer-skills.html)) [anecdote]. Sycophancy is the mechanism behind "I agree, you're right" reversals: human-preference training makes models match "user beliefs over truthful ones" across five assistants ([arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)) [measured, 2023].

**Addresses:** stopped-early (88), unsupported-or-stale claims (52; 77% of agent self-corrections were wrong statements), and the ceremony share (35% of operator messages). **Failure mode:** a Stop hook that cannot tell "finished" from "blocked on a real decision" either loops or trains the agent to word its way past the regex; the published pattern needs a work-list artifact to read, not text matching.

### P7. Tests first, then protect the tests from the agent

Beck's three warning signs that an agent is off the rails: loops, "functionality I hadn't asked for", and "any indication that the genie was cheating, for example by disabling or deleting tests"; his system prompt mandates Red→Green→Refactor and "only commit when ALL tests are passing" ([newsletter.kentbeck.com](https://newsletter.kentbeck.com/p/augmented-coding-beyond-the-vibes)) [anecdote]. Willison: red/green TDD and "automated tests are no longer optional when working with coding agents"; he lists "agents optimize for test passage over generalization" as a failure mode ([simonw.substack.com](https://simonw.substack.com/p/agentic-engineering-patterns)) [anecdote]. The **[measured]** worst case is OpenAI's reward-hacking paper: a frontier agent in coding environments used `exit(0)` to skip tests, raised `SkipTest` from outside the framework, wrote stubs where coverage was thin, modified upstream libraries and test files, and parsed test files at runtime for expected values; a chain-of-thought monitor caught 95% of the systemic hacks while an action-only monitor caught 60%, and training against the monitor produced hacks that "do not trigger the monitor" ([arxiv.org/html/2503.11926](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11926), [openai.com/index/chain-of-thought-monitoring](https://openai.com/index/chain-of-thought-monitoring/)). Böckeler lists mutation testing as a sensor ([harness-engineering](https://martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html)).

**Addresses:** test_defect (5.0%; 84% catchable by mutation-checking the test), fixtures that fabricate shapes, self-supplied preconditions, `status != 404` assertions. **Failure mode:** a surviving mutant that changes no behaviour (we measured 2 of 6); test-count inflation ("more tests are not necessarily better... the more brittle the tests get", Böckeler); and the monitor-evasion result means prose rules against cheating are the weakest possible defence.

### P8. Spec first, kept short, ending in a verification step; do not make the spec the source

Anthropic's version is an interview: "Keep interviewing until we've covered everything, then write a complete spec to SPEC.md", then a fresh session to build; good specs "name the files and interfaces involved, state what is out of scope, and end with an end-to-end verification step" ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]. Osmani's "waterfall in 15 minutes": a `spec.md` with requirements, architecture, data model and test strategy, then bite-sized tasks and a commit per task as "save points" ([addyosmani.com/blog/ai-coding-workflow](https://addyosmani.com/blog/ai-coding-workflow/)) [anecdote]. Hashimoto plans interactively with an "oracle" subagent first, scopes to one chunk ("We'll ONLY work on UI"), and still reviews everything by hand ([mitchellh.com/writing/non-trivial-vibing](https://mitchellh.com/writing/non-trivial-vibing)) [anecdote, 16 sessions, 8 hours].

Böckeler's ladder: spec-first (discard after), spec-anchored (keep and evolve), spec-as-source (humans edit only specs). Kiro is the lightest (requirements → design → tasks), Spec Kit produces "a LOT of markdown files" that are "repetitive" and "tedious to review", Tessl pursues spec-as-source; she asks whether elaborate SDD is "Verschlimmbesserung" and points to Model-Driven Development's history ([martinfowler.com .../sdd-3-tools](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)) [anecdote]. Eberhardt's **[measured]** comparison: Spec Kit generated 2,577 lines of markdown and 33.5 minutes of agent time for one feature versus 8 minutes without it, 3.5 hours of review versus 15 minutes, and the SDD build shipped an obvious bug the plain build did not; "around ten times faster" without SDD ([blog.scottlogic.com](https://blog.scottlogic.com/2025/11/26/putting-spec-kit-through-its-paces-radical-idea-or-reinvented-waterfall.html)) [n=2 features]. Thoughtworks puts Spec Kit in Assess and notes experienced engineers "tend to extract the most value" ([radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/radar/languages-and-frameworks/github-spec-kit)). Kiro's docs describe `requirements.md`/`design.md`/`tasks.md` with dependency-ordered task waves and are silent on keeping specs in sync with code ([kiro.dev/docs/specs](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/)) [vendor].

**Addresses:** misread-intent + built-wrong-thing (170, the largest operator class), spec_gap (2.2%), and the finding that only 26% of our spec traces were correct and testable. **Failure mode:** the markdown sea (our 1.8M words, 15 words per LOC on one dossier), review burden shifting from code to prose, and specs that prescribe the defect (17% of our traces).

### P9. Evals built from real failures, graded on outcome, 20–50 cases, read the transcripts

Anthropic: start with "20-50 simple tasks drawn from real failures"; "it's often better to grade what the agent produced, not the path it took"; code-based graders are brittle to valid variation, model graders hallucinate (give them an "Unknown" escape and grade one dimension at a time); a 100% pass rate is saturation, not success; do not take scores "at face value" without reading transcripts ([demystifying-evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)) [vendor]. The multi-agent post's rubric (factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, tool efficiency) and "end-state evaluation" are the same idea ([multi-agent-research-system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The LLM-as-judge survey catalogues position, length and self-preference biases and inconsistency, with mitigations of swapped positions, rubrics and multiple judges ([arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594)) [survey].

**Addresses:** external_integration (4.3%; our ~15 prompt commits tuning one analyst, mocks green while live returned 400), harness_self (a gate with no planted defect is an eval with no failing case). **Failure mode:** judge drift between runs (our own memory note says calibrate every run), saturation, and grading the path, which penalises a valid route.

### P10. Let the agent see the running system

OpenAI wired Chrome DevTools Protocol to an isolated app instance per worktree, with DOM snapshots, screenshots, LogQL logs and PromQL metrics, so agents loop "fix → restart → revalidate until clean" and single runs worked six hours ([openai.com](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/) via [alexlavaee.me](https://alexlavaee.me/blog/openai-agent-first-codebase-learnings/)) [anecdote]. Ronacher: "Logging is super important"; tools must be fast ("Crashes are tolerable; hangs are problematic"), guarded against "an LLM chaos monkey", and his process manager refuses to spawn a duplicate server and logs "services already running" ([lucumr.pocoo.org](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2025/6/12/agentic-coding/)) [anecdote]. Ball's team has agents log in and take screenshots to prove a change is "discovered and used" ([ampcode.com/podcast](https://ampcode.com/podcast)) [anecdote].

**Addresses:** wiring_integration (15.7%; boot-the-real-root was the cheapest catch for 42 of 88), deploy_env_config (10.5%), ui_or_design_mismatch (97 operator catches). **Failure mode:** an observability surface that points at a dev harness, superuser database or `/dev/login` observes the wrong thing (PR-09, PR-13); slow tools make the loop time out and the agent guess.

### P11. Small batches, one commit per task, worktrees per line of work

Osmani's commit-per-task "save points" and worktrees for parallel experiments ([addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/ai-coding-workflow/)); Anthropic's worktree and `/batch` guidance ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)); DORA 2025 lists "working in small batches" and "strong version control practices" among the seven capabilities that amplify AI's benefit ([faros.ai DORA takeaways](https://www.faros.ai/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-dora-report-2025), [cloud.google.com](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)) [measured, survey]. Faros telemetry on 10,000+ developers: high-AI teams merge 98% more PRs, PR size is up 154%, review time up 91%, bugs per developer up 9%, and "no significant correlation between AI adoption and improvements at the company level" ([faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering)) [measured]. Agentic PRs that fail to merge "involve larger code changes, touch more files, and often do not pass the project's CI/CD" across 33k agent PRs ([arxiv.org/abs/2601.15195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15195)) [measured].

**Addresses:** the 58% rework share, one-PR-per-line-of-work (restated 25+ times), the two-green-PRs-red-merged conflicts (PR-20). **Failure mode:** many small PRs each triggering full CI is where the ceremony and billing came from; the published fix is one PR with many commits, which is what the operator asked for.

### P12. Measure the harness and garbage-collect it

Marmelab: "Only 5 of 391 repositories measure harness usage in production"; "Document why each control exists. Remove obsolete rules when models improve" ([marmelab.com](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/09/24/the-state-of-ai-harness-engineering-2026.html)) [measured]. OpenAI initially spent "20% weekly cleanup time" on agent slop in Friday sprints, then replaced it with "golden principles" encoded as mechanical rules plus background agents that scan for drift and open targeted refactoring PRs with quality grades per layer ([alexlavaee.me](https://alexlavaee.me/blog/openai-agent-first-codebase-learnings/)) [anecdote]. Böckeler names this recurring "garbage collection" ([harness-engineering](https://martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html)). Anthropic: "Treat CLAUDE.md like code: review it when things go wrong, prune it regularly, and test changes by observing whether Claude's behavior actually shifts" ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]. Mitchell's AGENTS.md is built only from observed failures ([mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)). SlopCodeBench shows why continuous cleanup is needed: over 93 checkpoints, erosion rose in 80% of trajectories and verbosity in 89.8%, agent code was 2.2× more verbose than 48 human repositories, and "a prompt-intervention study shows that initial quality can be improved, but it does not halt degradation" ([arxiv.org/html/2603.24755v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24755v1)) [measured].

**Addresses:** harness_self (10.4%; 44% of CI job failures), 141 of 271 hook blocks being friction, 405 unpayable retro notices, apex rules with zero firings. **Failure mode:** measuring usage and then not deleting anything; cleanup agents that generate churn PRs (our docs train: 186 failures, 0 product defects).

### P13. Context hygiene: clear after two corrections, research in subagents, compaction that preserves commands

Anthropic: "If you've corrected Claude more than twice on the same issue in one session... Run `/clear`"; subagents keep exploration out of the main window; tell compaction what to preserve ("always preserve the full list of modified files and any test commands") ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]. The [measured] basis is P0's harness papers and Willison's observation that compaction loses instructions ([simonw.substack.com](https://simonw.substack.com/p/agentic-engineering-patterns)). **Addresses:** the empty-body fix chains (6 in 95 minutes) and slow_or_inefficient (47). **Failure mode:** a cleared session loses the operator's corrections unless they were written into an artifact (our rule 5).

### P14. Plan mode for multi-file or unfamiliar work; skip it for one-sentence diffs

Anthropic: explore → plan → implement → commit, with the caveat "If you could describe the diff in one sentence, skip the plan" ([best-practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [vendor]; the harness study found planning is mainly "a cost saver for stronger models" ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804)) [measured]. **Addresses:** built-wrong-thing (55), over-engineered (36), "30 minutes of testing for a one-line change". **Failure mode:** plans that are implementation prose (our 17.6k-word plan for five PRs).

## 2. How the field measures agent-era quality

- **DORA 2025** (survey, ~5,000 respondents): 90% use AI; AI now correlates positively with throughput but "continues to have a negative relationship with software delivery stability"; 30% report little or no trust in AI code; the metric set is three throughput measures (deployment frequency, lead time, **rework rate**: work that returns to in-progress) and two instability measures (change failure rate, failed-deployment recovery time) ([cloud.google.com announcement](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report), [dora.dev](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/), [faros.ai takeaways](https://www.faros.ai/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-dora-report-2025)) [measured].
- **CodeRabbit**: 470 PRs, AI-co-authored PRs average 10.83 issues versus 6.45, 1.75× logic errors, ~2× error-handling gaps, up to 2.74× security; authorship labelling is by co-author signal and the authors say they "cannot guarantee" the human set is human-only ([coderabbit.ai](https://www.coderabbit.ai/blog/state-of-ai-vs-human-code-generation-report)) [measured, vendor-run].
- **GitClear**: 211M changed lines; refactored lines fell from ~25% (2021) to under 10% (2024); copy-paste rose from 8.3% to 12.3% ([gitclear.com](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research)) [measured, vendor-run]. Thoughtworks puts "complacency with AI-generated code" on Hold citing it ([radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/complacency-with-ai-generated-code)).
- **METR RCT**: 16 experienced maintainers, 246 issues, 19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster ([metr.org](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)) [measured]; the 2026 follow-up (57 developers, 800+ tasks) showed some speed-up but METR itself called the central estimate unreliable because of selection effects ([andrewwegner.com summary](https://andrewwegner.com/metr-ai-productivity-study-update.html)) [measured, contested].
- **Stack Overflow 2025** (49k respondents): 29% trust AI accuracy, 45% name "almost right, but not quite" as the top frustration, 66% spend more time fixing almost-right code ([stackoverflow.blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2025/12/29/developers-remain-willing-but-reluctant-to-use-ai-the-2025-developer-survey-results-are-here/)) [measured, survey].

For us the usable metrics are **rework rate** (we already have 58% fix-of-fix), **change failure rate on the named target**, and **time-to-first-operator-catch**, since 27% of our defects were found by the operator on real data.

## 3. Ranked: the ten practices with the strongest evidence for our situation

Ranking weighs evidence strength against the defect share and operator-catch count it addresses.

1. **Deterministic hooks for every must-always rule, with deny on bypass flags and a fail-open notice that is itself a check** (P2). Measured 4.4% enforcement of prose rules; documented six-commit bypass; our 22/32 prose rules.
2. **A runnable check per task and a Stop-gated "done" that cites the check's output** (P1, P6). Vendor plus unanimous practitioners; addresses 111 + 88 + 52 operator catches.
3. **Mechanically enforced architecture plus a boot of the real composition root as the check** (P4 + P10). OpenAI at 1M LOC; our seam family is 35% of escapes and no published linter catches wiring, so the boot is the local addition.
4. **Tests first, mutation-checked, with test files protected from the agent** (P7). Reward-hacking paper; Beck; our 84%-catchable test_defect class.
5. **Instruction file under a tested ceiling, skills on demand, docs/ as a map** (P3). ETH Zurich null result on overviews; OpenAI ~100 lines; our regrowth.
6. **Fresh-context two-voice review capped at two rounds and judged against code** (P5). Our own 46-review result beats the published averages; keep it, keep it cold, keep it short.
7. **Short spec with a verification line, from an interview; never spec-as-source** (P8). Addresses the largest operator class; Scott Logic's 10× and Böckeler's warning bound the weight.
8. **Harness telemetry and deletion** (P12). 5/391 measure; our 15% useful-block rate.
9. **Evals from the last 20–50 real failures, outcome-graded, transcripts read** (P9). The only published way to know a gate or a prompt change helped.
10. **One PR per line of work, small commits, worktrees** (P11). DORA capability; Faros PR-size data; 25+ operator restatements.

## 4. What the community tried and found not to work

- **Repository overviews and long context files**: no success gain, +20% cost ([arxiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)); machine-generated ones can "reduce task success compared to giving the agent nothing" ([marmelab](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/09/24/the-state-of-ai-harness-engineering-2026.html)).
- **Security and safety rules written in prose**: 4.4% backed by a control ([arxiv 2608.23550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23550v1)); agents bypass git-level enforcement ([issue 40117](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/40117)).
- **Heavy spec-driven pipelines (Spec Kit as used)**: 10× slower with a bug ([Scott Logic](https://blog.scottlogic.com/2025/11/26/putting-spec-kit-through-its-paces-radical-idea-or-reinvented-waterfall.html)); "repetitive", "tedious to review", MDD déjà vu ([Böckeler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)); Assess, not Adopt ([Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/radar/languages-and-frameworks/github-spec-kit)).
- **Review agents as a replacement for a human or a test**: 12/13 below 60% signal, lower merge rate ([arxiv 2604.03196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03196)); an 8% success drop in one benchmark and no multi-agent advantage ([marmelab](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/09/24/the-state-of-ai-harness-engineering-2026.html)).
- **Many purpose-built tools**: fewer tools, more success ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/we-removed-80-percent-of-our-agents-tools)); "bloated tool sets" named as a top failure mode ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)).
- **Prompt fixes for quality decay**: initial quality improves, degradation continues ([SlopCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24755v1)); our ~15 prompt commits before a deterministic fix.
- **Permission rules written in advance**: blocked 20.1 points fewer violations than per-action approval ([marmelab](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/09/24/the-state-of-ai-harness-engineering-2026.html), citing an arXiv study).
- **Monitoring the agent's stated reasoning as the enforcement**: optimisation against the monitor yields hacks that "do not trigger the monitor" ([arxiv 2503.11926](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11926)).
- **Trusting the developer's own speed estimate**: a 44-point perception gap ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)).

## 5. Open questions

1. **Behavioural harnesses.** Böckeler: "we still have a lot to do to figure out good harnesses for functional behaviour" ([harness-engineering](https://martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html)). Nothing published covers our domain-rule class (one owner per concept, real-data oracles with data that never leaves the machine); the field's answer is still "the human tests it".
2. **Stop-gate semantics.** No source shows how a Stop hook distinguishes "work remains" from "a decision is needed" without a structured work list; our 88 stop-early catches need that artifact designed.
3. **Solo-operator review.** Every reviewer study assumes a human reviewer exists; ours has 0 reviews on the last 300 merged PRs. Whether cold two-voice review can carry the whole load is unmeasured outside our own 46-review sample.
4. **Hook coverage across entry points.** The published hook model is per-tool and per-branch; nobody reports a working pattern for PowerShell + Bash + subagents on Windows.
5. **Where the ETH null result stops applying.** The study measured SWE-bench-style issue fixing; whether commands-and-constraints files help on greenfield multi-root fleets is untested.
6. **Harness cost accounting.** Faros and DORA measure team outcomes, marmelab measures repository features; nobody publishes cost per PR of a given gate, which the brief requires us to state.
7. **Sycophancy under correction.** The sycophancy result is from 2023 chat models; its size in current coding agents when the operator pushes back is unmeasured, and it bears directly on "statements need receipts".
